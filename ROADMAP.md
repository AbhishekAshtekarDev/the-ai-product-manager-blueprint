# The twelve-month roadmap from zero to hired

From Chapter 43 of **[The AI Product Manager Blueprint](https://theaiproductmanagerblueprint.com/)**, the best book to become an AI Product Manager. Twelve months is a planning model, a sensible order for the work, so move at the pace your background allows. Fork this repo and tick the boxes as you go.

## Months 1 to 3: the role, product fundamentals and AI foundations

### Month 1: understand the role and the market

- [ ] Learn what AI Product Managers actually do and how the role connects users, business goals, design, engineering, data and responsible AI
- [ ] Read twenty real AI PM job descriptions, including adjacent titles, and record repeated skills and evidence employers ask for
- [ ] Write your own definition of the role and pick an initial direction: AI-enabled, applied AI or core AI PM work

### Month 2: learn product management fundamentals

- [ ] Break down one product you use often: user, job, core loop, value, friction, business model and likely metrics
- [ ] Run two or three short user interviews about behaviour and workarounds, not feature ideas
- [ ] Compare three products in one AI category and write two one-page PRDs

### Month 3: learn AI and machine learning foundations

- [ ] Learn the difference between deterministic rules and probabilistic systems
- [ ] Learn the generative AI vocabulary: tokens, context, embeddings, retrieval, hallucination, fine-tuning and tools
- [ ] Take ten product problems, decide which ones need AI, and write down why

## Months 4 to 6: modern AI and product judgment

### Month 4: build data literacy, SQL and analytics

- [ ] Learn enough SQL to select, filter, group, aggregate and join data to answer product questions
- [ ] Learn activation, engagement, retention, conversion, churn, funnels and cohorts

### Month 5: study LLMs, retrieval, agents and prototyping

- [ ] Learn what language models do well and how prompts and context shape output
- [ ] Learn where retrieval grounds answers and how tool-using agents create value and risk
- [ ] Prototype with no-code and low-code AI tools

### Month 6: practise evaluation, responsible AI and product sense

- [ ] Define evaluations for several products and show that "good" changes by task
- [ ] Practise the trade-offs between quality, cost, latency, trust and safety

## Months 7 to 9: turn learning into visible work

### Month 7: build the first two portfolio projects

- [ ] Build an AI Interview Coach
- [ ] Build a Voice of Customer Co-Pilot

### Month 8: add two projects and publish a portfolio

- [ ] Rebuild a real product feature with AI
- [ ] Build a Meeting-to-Execution Co-Pilot
- [ ] Publish a simple portfolio website with a case study for each project

### Month 9: complete the fifth project and your job-search materials

- [ ] Build a RAG-based knowledge base assistant with sources, citations, permissions and refusal
- [ ] Finish an ATS-friendly resume, a cover letter template and your LinkedIn profile

## Months 10 to 12: run the application and interview campaign

### Month 10: start a targeted application campaign

- [ ] Build a weekly pipeline of tailored applications, conversations, referrals and follow-ups
- [ ] Split roles into realistic entry points, stretch roles and dream roles
- [ ] Track response rates by role type, seniority, source and resume version

### Month 11: prepare for interviews and portfolio walkthroughs

- [ ] Practise product sense, metrics, strategy, technical AI, behavioural and take-home questions
- [ ] Rehearse a walkthrough of every portfolio project

### Month 12: improve conversion and evaluate offers

- [ ] Find the weakest stage of your funnel and fix it instead of restarting
- [ ] Negotiate the offer and plan your first ninety days
